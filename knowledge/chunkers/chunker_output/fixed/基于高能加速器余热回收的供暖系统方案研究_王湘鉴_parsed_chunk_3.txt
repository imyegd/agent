Large-scale research infrastructures, such as high-energy particle accelerators, represent concentrated energy consumption nodes, with much of their electricity input ultimately rejected as low-grade thermal energy through cooling towers. Harnessing this largely untapped waste heat for space heating presents a dual opportunity: improving the energy efficiency of major scientific facilities while displacing carbon-intensive heating sources. This study addresses the specific technical and operational challenges of recovering low-grade, intermittent waste heat from an operational accelerator, using the Beijing Electron-Positron Collider (BEPC) as a representative case to develop and comprehensively evaluate a practical heating solution.
