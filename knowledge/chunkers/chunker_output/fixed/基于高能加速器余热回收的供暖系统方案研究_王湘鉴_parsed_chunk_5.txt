The heating load of the target buildings － a multidisciplinary research building and a guest house totaling $2 4 , 8 2 2  { \mathrm { m } } ^ { 2 }$ － was calculated using standard thermal index methods, yielding a demand of $1 { , } 2 4 1 { . } 1 \mathrm { k W }$ , which is fully covered by the available waste heat. Subsequently, an integrated system was designed, centering on a screw-type compression heat pump. This technology was selected after a detailed comparison with centrifugal and absorption alternatives, with decision factors emphasizing excellent part-load performance, tolerance to source temperature variation, and favorable lifecycle costs. The system architecture prioritizes accelerator operational safety, incorporates redundancy with a backup connection to the campus heating network, and was spatially planned using Building Information Modeling (BIM) for optimal layout.
