Abstract： This study analyzes the influencing factors of the automatic positioning accuracy of a 6D linear accelerator (LINAC) treatment couch. It discusses error measurement, calibration model construction, and calibration algorithm optimization, proposing an automatic positioning accuracy calibration technique based on multi-sensor fusion and iterative optimization. Through high-precision error measurement experiments, error compensation model development, and adaptive calibration algorithm design, the study achieves effective detection and correction of 6D couch positioning errors. Results demonstrate that the proposed technique reduces the average translational positioning errors $( X , Y , Z )$ from 0.8 mm to below 0.2 mm and rotational errors (Pitch, Roll, Yaw) from $0 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ to below $0 . 1 5 ^ { \circ }$ , significantly improving radiotherapy targeting accuracy and treatment efficacy. This approach effectively addresses the precision limitations of traditional positioning methods.

Key words： linear accelerator; 6D treatment couch; automatic positioning accuracy; calibration technique
