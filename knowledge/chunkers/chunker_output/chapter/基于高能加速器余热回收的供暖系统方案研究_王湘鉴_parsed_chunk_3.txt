# Heating System Based on High Energy Accelerator Waste Heat Recovery

WANG Xiangjian△, SU Liuwen, JIN Huanhuan

(Supporting Services Centre, Institute of High Energy Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China)

Abstract: [Objective] Amidst China’s national strategy to achieve carbon peak and carbon neutrality, the transition towards clean, low-carbon heating systems has become a critical priority. This shift is especially urgent in northern regions where conventional fossil fuel-based heating impacts significantly to carbon emissions and air pollution. Large-scale research infrastructures, such as high-energy particle accelerators, represent concentrated energy consumption nodes, with much of their electricity input ultimately rejected as low-grade thermal energy through cooling towers. Harnessing this largely untapped waste heat for space heating presents a dual opportunity: improving the energy efficiency of major scientific facilities while displacing carbon-intensive heating sources. This study addresses the specific technical and operational challenges of recovering low-grade, intermittent waste heat from an operational accelerator, using the Beijing Electron-Positron Collider (BEPC) as a representative case to develop and comprehensively evaluate a practical heating solution. The research is motivated by the significant thermal resource available at facilities like BEPC and the pressing need to decarbonize municipal heating system. While international examples such as CERN demonstrate the principle, applying waste heat recovery to existing, medium-scale accelerators with variable operational schedules poses distinct challenges. This study explicitly targets three core problems: (1) accurately quantifying the available waste heat potential and matching its intermittent profile with stable building heating demand; (2) designing a non-intrusive system integration scheme that guarantees zero interference with the accelerator’s primary scientific operations; and (3) conducting a holistic techno-economic-environmental assessment to verify the project’s viability and provide a replicable evaluation framework. [Methods] The methodology follows a systematic, data-driven approach. First, BEPC’s waste heat resource was quantified through analysis of design specifications and operational monitoring data from the 2024-2025 heating season, confirming a stable low-grade source at approximately $3 2 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with a recoverable power of about 2.1 MW. The heating load of the target buildings － a multidisciplinary research building and a guest house totaling $2 4 , 8 2 2  { \mathrm { m } } ^ { 2 }$ － was calculated using standard thermal index methods, yielding a demand of $1 { , } 2 4 1 { . } 1 \mathrm { k W }$ , which is fully covered by the available waste heat. Subsequently, an integrated system was designed, centering on a screw-type compression heat pump. This technology was selected after a detailed comparison with centrifugal and absorption alternatives, with decision factors emphasizing excellent part-load performance, tolerance to source temperature variation, and favorable lifecycle costs. The system architecture prioritizes accelerator operational safety, incorporates redundancy with a backup connection to the campus heating network, and was spatially planned using Building Information Modeling (BIM) for optimal layout. A comprehensive evaluation model was then constructed, integrating thermodynamic performance (Coefficient of Performance, COP), full lifecycle economic analysis (including capital investment, operational savings, and payback period), and carbon emission reduction calculations. Finally, sensitivity analysis via tornado charts and Monte Carlo simulation was employed to identify and

quantify the key factors driving economic uncertainty. [Results] The results demonstrate a technically robust and economically attractive proposal. The designed system achieves a high heating COP of 5.0. Economically, with a total initial investment estimated at 3.224 million RMB, the system generates annual operational cost savings of approximately 919,000 RMB compared to a conventional gas boiler baseline, leading to a short static payback period of 3.5 years － a period that could be further reduced under Beijing’s subsidy policies for renewable heating. In each heating season, the system is projected to save 260.5 tons of standard coal equivalent and reduce $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ emissions by 692.9 tons. Sensitivity analysis clearly identifies the operational load factor (directly affecting electricity consumption) and the electricity price as the two dominant economic variables, jointly accounting for over $9 5 \%$ of the variance in annual costs. A comparative summary against gas and electric boiler systems underscores the WHR system’s superior energy efficiency, lower operating cost, and significantly reduced carbon intensity. [Conclusions] In conclusion, this study successfully develops, designs, and validates a waste heat recovery heating system tailored for the BEPC. It confirms the technical feasibility and substantial economic and environmental benefits of utilizing stable, low-grade accelerator waste heat for building comfort heating without compromising scientific operations. The work provides a complete and systematic technical pathway － from resource assessment and technology selection to integrated design and multi-criteria evaluation － that can serve as a replicable blueprint for energy-saving retrofits in similar large-scale scientific infrastructures both in China and internationally. The findings offer a quantifiable, practical contribution to the green transformation of major research campuses and support the broader national goals of energy conservation, emission reduction, and sustainable development. Future work should focus on operational performance validation through long-term monitoring and investigate strategies such as thermal energy storage to further enhance system flexibility and annual utilization.

Keywords: high energy particle accelerator; waste heat recovery; building heating; low grade heat source; technoeconomic analysis; heat pump technology
